[rules] What is stalemate, and how is it different from checkmate?
============================================================================================

  ALONE
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Stalemate occurs when a player has no legal moves and their king is not in check, meaning they can't make a move but aren't in immediate danger. In contrast, checkmate happens when a player's king is in check and there is no legal move to escape the threat, resulting in the player's defeat. Stalemate results in a draw, while checkmate ends the game with a win for the opponent. Understanding both situations is crucial for strategic play and avoiding unnecessary losses.

============================================================================================

Rapid games allow for more strategic thinking and deeper calculation compared to blitz. In rapid, players have more time to think through their moves and plan ahead, while blitz focuses more on quick decision-making. Rapid games are often considered a middle ground between blitz and classical chess.

[rules] When can I capture en passant?
============================================================================================

  ALONE
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    You can capture en passant when your opponent moves a pawn two squares forward from its starting position, and it lands beside one of your pawns. This capture must occur immediately on the very next move. The capturing pawn moves diagonally into the square that the opponent's pawn would have occupied if it had moved only one square forward instead. Remember, en passant is only possible in the specific situation described.

  WITH RETRIEVAL (1 passages)
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  ALONE
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    A backward pawn is a pawn that is isolated and lacks support from its own pieces, usually positioned behind its own pawns. It is a weakness because it can become vulnerable to attacks and difficult to defend, limiting the player's options and weakening their overall position.

[concept] Why are doubled pawns often considered weak?
============================================================================================

  ALONE
  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Doubled pawns are often considered weak because they limit the mobility of your pawns, making it harder to support each other and defend your position. They also create weaknesses that your opponent can exploit, as they are harder to protect and can become isolated. Additionally, having doubled pawns can hinder your ability to create a cohesive pawn structure, which is crucial for maintaining control over the center and supporting your pieces. It's generally better to avoid creating doubled pawns unless they are strategically necessary.
