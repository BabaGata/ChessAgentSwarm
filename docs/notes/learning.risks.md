# Risks, Issues & Prevention

Two sections: **anticipated risks** (not yet materialised) and **encountered issues** (they did).
Every encountered issue must end with a prevention measure that changes something concrete — a rule
in [[process]], a check in the `adaptive-cycle` skill, a test, or a design constraint.

## Anticipated risks

| ID | Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Prevention / early warning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-01 | **Cost creep** — the design quietly becomes LLM-call-heavy and violates C1 | high | high | Cost per operation is estimated at design time and measured in [[evaluation]]; D9 scorecard dimension makes it visible every cycle |
| R-02 | **Unfalsifiable coaching output** — the swarm produces plausible advice nobody can verify | high | high | V8 explainability is a vision capability, not a nice-to-have; every recommendation must cite evidence from the player's own games |
| R-03 | **Folklore laundering** — LLM-generated chess advice presented as expert knowledge | high | medium | Evidence-class marking in [[capacity.knowledge]]; sources recorded in [[domain.sources]] |
| R-04 | **Agent sprawl** — one agent per section produces a swarm too large to orchestrate or evaluate | medium | medium | M2 orders sections by priority; build agents in priority order and re-assess the swarm each M5 rather than building all of them |
| R-05 | **Scope drift into engine work** | medium | medium | Explicit non-goal in [[vision]]; use existing engines |
| R-06 | **Documentation rot** — notes describe an intended system, not the real one | high | medium | [[state]] is rewritten every cycle with evidence; the cycle does not close without it |
| R-07 | **Codebase getting out of hand** | medium | medium | Refactoring assessment is a mandatory step of every cycle before commit ([[process]]) |
| R-08 | **Thesis-time overrun** — infinite refinement of the loop instead of building | high | medium | Each mission step has a definition of done; C6 requires each step to end on something usable |
| R-09 | **Evaluation without ground truth** — no way to tell good coaching from bad | high | high | [[evaluation]] must define proxies (engine agreement, rating trajectory, expert review) before M4 builds anything |
| R-10 | **Player data privacy** — handling real players' games and profiles | medium | low | Use public games only; no personal data beyond public usernames; state this in the thesis |
| R-11 | **Weak source base** — chess-improvement web content is largely commercial marketing of variable rigour, and some of it sells the very thing it recommends studying | high | **materialised in M1, largely mitigated 2026-08-14** | Evidence class recorded per source in [[domain.sources]]; commercial bias noted inline. **The load-bearing decisions are now traced to peer-reviewed work** read directly from the PDF → [[domain.expertise-research]], and it supported them rather than overturning them. Two residual exposures, both stated: the **four-way gap taxonomy** drives the schema and still has no traced source, and the literature is **contested on whether coaching helps at all** — which a thesis building a coach must answer rather than omit |
| R-12 | **LLM coaching default is the anti-pattern** — asked to coach, a language model lists many weaknesses and recites generic level-appropriate advice, which is exactly what [[domain.coaching]] § 4 identifies as bad coaching | high | high | Design against it explicitly: the swarm must output **one or two** priorities with evidence; add this to the agent efficacy measures in [[evaluation]] |
| R-17 | **A layer of the opening design depends on a third-party service that is down** — `explorer.lichess.ovh` returns 401 for every request and has been unresponsive since a February 2026 infrastructure incident. The API specification says no authentication is needed, which is true and useless while the service is unreachable | medium | **materialised 2026-08-23** | Layer 3 stays deferred and the design carries on without it, which it can. More generally: **check that a proposed dependency is alive, not only that it is documented and free** — the specification and the service are different objects, and a search summary describes the first |
| R-16 | **Detector correctness is invisible to the test suite** — the motifs encode chess judgements, and a green suite says only that they still do what they did yesterday. 1040 tests passed while every Black move was cited a number too high and the fork detector had no notion of piece values (L-044, D17) | **high** | **materialised 2026-08-23** | Precision per detector, measured on hand-verified samples, is the only evidence that counts — the method [[experiments.e04-motif-precision]] used. Re-screen after any change to a detector, and treat an expert reading real output as a **required** instrument rather than a final validation step: it is the only thing that has ever caught this class of defect here |
| R-15 | **Regression to the mean makes any coaching system look effective** — a weakness is diagnosed because its rate was extreme, and re-measuring an extreme value returns a lower one whether or not anything was done | **critical** | **materialised and mitigated** | Measured in E05 at 92 % of targets met with no intervention. **Now 15 %, held out** (57 predictions, 84 players, cross-validated at 2 and 5 folds, spread 0.011). Targets are calibrated against the measured no-change distribution rather than the selected value, and every progress sign states how often doing nothing would suffice. **Two things keep this open.** The effect's *size* turned out to depend on how thinly the finding was measured — +11.2 points at 60-game histories, +4.8 at 150 (L-019) — so the constant is not a fixed property and must be refitted whenever the sample changes (**D9**). And only the false-positive side is measured: nothing shows a coached player could clear the bar (**D8**) |
| R-14 | **Correct but irrelevant output** — the swarm states things that are true, specific, evidence-backed and worthless. No correctness test catches this, because nothing is wrong | high | **materialised twice: E02 and M5** | Predicted from base rates in E02; **occurred on real data in M5**, where four of six players showed the same "weakness" at similar magnitude because the condition was selected by the thing causing the error (L-011). Mitigations now in force: selection-confounded conditions are withheld pending a peer baseline; the peer corpus is promoted to blocking; evaluation metric D1 (inter-player divergence) is promoted so the next instance is caught automatically rather than by eye |
| R-13 | **Statistical overclaiming** — declaring a weakness from a handful of games, or comparing ACPL across time controls and opponents | high | **materialised in E03 and caught** | Minimum-sample and confidence rules ([[domain.signals]] § 4, question C2). **Demonstrated:** E03's phase-stratified backward-pawn effect (lift 2.40) looked convincing, had a tidy mechanism, and reversed to 0.76 on held-out players. Mandatory control: every discovered association reproduces on a *different player set* before being written down (L-008) |

## Encountered issues

| ID | Date | Issue | Root cause | Fix | Prevention applied |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I-01** | 2026-08-04 | A live probe session recorded every reason as *unclear*. The player's answer plainly described a pin, and the profile said the system could not tell | **Ollama had stopped.** `_post` returned `None` on a connection failure, and `None` was also the legitimate value for "the model replied but not usably" — so an unreachable backend was indistinguishable, in the stored profile, from a vague player | `ClassifierUnavailable` is raised rather than returned; `interpret` catches it and records `classifier_status`, which separates *answered / unclear / declined / not_consulted / not_configured / unavailable* (schema v5) | The session now **warns on screen** when the classifier was unreachable, `ProbeRecord.was_actually_asked` lets anything reasoning over probes exclude ones never really asked, and six tests pin the distinction. General form recorded as **L-020** |
| **I-03** | 2026-08-08 | A player whose recent games were **entirely blitz** had their style compared against the **rapid** population | `style.describe` looked up one *stated* time control rather than the player's speed mix. Style is a profile field, not a finding, so it never passes through `SectionContext` — and step 5's mix-matching was built there. The fix went where the code went, not where the comparison happened | `describe` takes an optional `speed_mix` and weights the population across the speeds actually played, exactly as `SectionContext._mixed` does | Two tests pin an all-blitz and a mixed player. The general form: **a cross-cutting fix must be applied at every place that makes the comparison, not at every place that shares a call path.** Found only by running a live session — the corpus players all had rapid histories, so no test or experiment could surface it |
| **I-04** | 2026-08-08 | The report stated *"About 1645"* as a rating for a corpus of **only blitz games**, with no qualification | V1 was fitted on rapid games (E13). Admitting blitz in step 5 made an out-of-calibration corpus reachable for the first time, and nothing connected the two — the estimator does not know what it was fitted on | **Refit, same day.** Blitz has its own line — `1841.3 − 12045.1 × blunder_rate`, held out **±123** against rapid's ±103 — the estimate is per speed and **names** which, and `MIN_MOVES` applies *within* that speed so a genuinely split player is refused rather than handed an average of two rating scales | The stop-gap caveat became a statement of which reading is weaker. 18 tests. **The general form: admitting new data to a corpus silently moves every model fitted on the old corpus out of calibration, and nothing warns you — the estimator does not know what it was fitted on.** Any future widening of the corpus must re-ask which fits it invalidates |
| **I-02** | 2026-08-04 | A correct move was recorded as wrong, manufacturing a knowledge gap the player did not have | A byte-order mark on piped input. `str.strip()` does not remove `﻿`, because Python does not classify it as whitespace | `_normalise()` strips invisible characters before comparing moves | Six parametrised tests covering BOM, zero-width space and combinations. Noticed only because the first live session was checked against what the report said the better move was — **running it for real is what found both of these** |
| **I-05** | 2026-08-29 | The detection sheet was **scored against detector code two commits old**, so marks the author would have made described behaviour that no longer existed | E55 added a commit stamp to the sheet precisely so marks could be dated — and then nothing read it. A stamp is only a guard if something refuses on it; otherwise it is a comment | `score.py` computes the current stamp of every detector file and **refuses** a sheet whose detectors have changed since, naming the commits and requiring `--allow-stale` to override | The general form: **an artefact that records provenance but is never checked against it provides no protection, only the feeling of it.** Any future stamp gets its refusal in the same commit |
| **I-06** | 2026-08-30 | The knowledge swarm's relevance gate passed **four pages with nothing to do with chess** — a Beyoncé film, a Go wiki, a constructed-language grammar and a libertarian-communism essay — into the drafting pool | The gate short-circuited on the **title** containing a claim word, and the words are ordinary English: "fork", "pin", "hanging". A title match was treated as sufficient rather than as one weak signal | A URL-structural check plus a chess-density floor (**1 mention per 5 KB of text**), with the title no longer able to short-circuit anything | Four negative fixtures, one per leaked page. The general form: **a filter keyed on a word that is ordinary in another domain will admit that domain**, and it will do so silently because every admitted page looks locally plausible |
| **I-07** | 2026-08-31 | The regenerated detection sheet listed the four new development claims as **NEVER FIRED** while the pipeline was firing them normally | Two key formats. The claims are stored as `kind.subject.own` and the sheet's tally was built on `kind.subject`, so the lookup missed and **the miss returned the same empty tally as a genuine no-fire** | Keys are normalised through a single `_key()` on both sides | **L-046, eighth instance** — an empty case answering exactly like a populated one. Prevention is structural rather than a test: one function owns the key format, so the two sides cannot drift apart again. Found by reading the sheet against a run, not by the suite |
