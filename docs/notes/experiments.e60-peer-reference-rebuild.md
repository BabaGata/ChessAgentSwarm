# E60 — Rebuilding the peer reference, and the wall the claims hit

**Answers:** [[design.opening-development-signals]] · **Code:** `chesscoach/opening_development.py`,
`chesscoach/development_norms.py`, `experiments/e58-opening-development/peer_check.py` ·
**Date:** 2026-08-30 · **Status:** **claims work, and cannot be delivered** — an arbiter decision the
author has to make

## What was built

The three surviving claims needed to exist as `ConditionMeasurement`s before any peer comparison was
possible, so they were wired into **S4**, which is where `early_error` — the claim they replace —
already lives.

- **`chesscoach/development_norms.py`** — the strong-player expectation as a loadable artefact,
  `data/openings/development-norms.json`, 232 cells and 86 with 20+ games. Same shape as the opening
  book: a load format, not a query format. Regenerated by `build_norms.py`.
- **`chesscoach/opening_development.py`** — tallies the three claims, keeping the two bases in
  **separate claim keys** (`.book` vs `.own`) so *"late by this opening's standard"* and *"later here
  than in your own other openings"* can never be pooled into one number.
- **Games are rebuilt from the observations**, not from PGN: every ply gets an observation, so
  grouping by game and sorting by ply reproduces the move list exactly and no new data path is
  needed.

**Reference rebuilt across both strata**, 112 cells:

| stratum | cells | players per development cell |
|---|--:|--:|
| rapid | 56 | 67–71 |
| blitz | 56 | 62–65 |

## Two bugs, and the first one is the interesting one

**The claims produced perfect numbers and said nothing.** `_assess` refuses any claim with no
`examples`, and the development tallies carried none — so every claim was dropped **silently**, with
nothing wrong in any count.

The refusal is correct: **V8 requires every claim to cite the player's own games**, and a claim with
no move to point at is exactly what the rule exists to stop. The fault was mine for not supplying
one. Each instance now cites the move that decided it — the castling move, or the player's last
opening move when they never castled, which is the honest evidence for *"and here the king was still
at home"*. Three tests pin it, and they exist because the failure was invisible.

**The second bug was mine in the harness.** The first peer check reported a clean **0 of 12** and I
nearly wrote it up as a finding. It was an artefact: I built a **rapid-only** reference, and **10 of
the 12 review players are blitz-dominant**, so every lookup fell through the missing stratum and
returned `None`. Instrumenting one player before reporting the number is the only reason it was
caught — the same habit that found the checkmate fork and E56's empty baseline.

## Do the claims separate 1600 from 1600?

This is the question everything before it could not answer: the expectation comes from 2600s, but the
claim compares a player with **their own band**.

**Spread within the band** — per-player rates from the reference itself:

| claim | players | p10 | median | p90 | spread |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| `late_castling.book` | 69 | 16 % | 43 % | 67 % | **51 pp** |
| `slow_development.book` | 69 | 27 % | 48 % | 73 % | **45 pp** |
| `repeat_move.any` | 71 | 33 % | 44 % | 57 % | 24 pp |

**How many could actually clear the priority margin** (1.25× the pooled rate):

| claim | rapid | blitz | median opportunities |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| `slow_development.book` | **18/69** | 14/61 | 22 |
| `late_castling.book` | **16/69** | 19/61 | 22 |
| `repeat_move.any` | 7/71 | 4/65 | 448 |

**About a quarter of the band is meaningfully worse than its peers** on the two headline claims. They
discriminate within band, which is what the whole design needed and what no earlier measurement had
shown.

**`repeat_move` is much weaker** — 6–10 % of players clear the margin despite having twenty times the
opportunities. That matches its halved effect size in E59 and is the honest picture of it.

## And then the wall

| | |
|---|--:|
| review players with a development claim **asserted** | **1 / 12** |
| with one **watched** | 2 / 12 |
| with one **in their plan** | **0 / 12** |

The zero is not marginal and not about evidence. `arbiter._sort_key` ranks findings by tier, then:

```python
0 if cost is not None else 1,
```

**A claim that cannot state a cost sorts below every claim that can.** Development claims have no
cost by construction — developing slowly is a habit, not a move that lost measurable win probability,
and `_count_development` deliberately declines to invent one.

This is a deliberate design, argued in S5's design note and quoted in the arbiter itself: *"a claim
that cannot say what it costs should have to work harder for one of a player's two slots."* But with
three slots and a supply of costed claims that is never empty, **"work harder" is in practice "never
win"**. The claims can be true, asserted, and unreachable.

## Consequence — and the decision that is the author's

The measurement works. The delivery does not, and the fix is a judgement about what a coaching plan
is for:

1. **Give the development claims a cost.** Price the win probability lost over the opening window in
   games where development was slow. **Risky**: slow development correlates with being worse
   generally, so the cost would capture the player's whole weakness and overstate what fixing this
   would recover.
2. **Let a costless claim compete on unusualness.** Change the arbiter so cost breaks ties rather
   than dominating. This touches the ranking of every claim in the system and would need E17's
   measurement re-run.
3. **Reserve a slot for a habit claim.** Explicitly, so one plan step can be a behaviour rather than
   a priced error.

**No option is obviously right and all three change what every player is told**, so none is taken
here.

## Honest limitations

- **1 of 12 is a small number to reason from.** The band-level figure says ~25 % should clear the
  margin, which predicts about 3 of 12; getting 1 is within noise for a sample this size, and the
  gap between "clears the margin" and "reaches a tier" also includes distinct-game floors and
  interval width that this note did not separate.
- **The band is one band.** Everything here is `1400-1800`.
- **The expectation is still elite-derived**, so part of every gap is strength rather than knowledge.
  The peer step was supposed to net that out and it has not been shown to.
- **Nothing has been read by a person.** No development claim has been checked against the games it
  would be made about, which is the same debt the fork sample is waiting on.
