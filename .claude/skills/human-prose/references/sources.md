than in Croatian, and the sentence-level habits carry over even though the
examples do not.

## The caution, which matters

Every source says the same thing: none of these markers proves anything. Academic
prose legitimately shares features with model output — formal tone, hedging,
symmetry, low personal voice — so this is a **false-positive-prone** genre.

Two consequences for how the skill is used:

1. The target is not "pass a detector". It is that the thesis reads like the
   person who wrote it. Density against **the author's own baseline** is the
   measure, not any absolute threshold.
2. Nothing here justifies removing hedges, negative results, or stated
   limitations. Those are the argument. Remove the mannerism, keep the honesty.

## The Croatian-specific part, which the English sources do not cover

The em-dash aside (`riječ --- objašnjenje --- nastavak`) is a normal English
construction and an unusual Croatian one. Croatian writers reach for a comma,
a relative clause (`a to je`, `pri čemu`, `koji`), or brackets. So the same
habit that reads as merely dense in English reads as foreign in Croatian, which
is why it was the first thing the author noticed.

The author's measured rate, over her two earlier reports: **4 em dashes in 4,039
words**, and two of those are in a figure caption rather than in prose.
