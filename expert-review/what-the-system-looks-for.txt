WHAT THE SYSTEM LOOKS FOR
==========================================================================

Generated from the code, so it is what the swarm actually measures rather
than what anyone remembers it measuring.

Read this BEFORE annotating, not after. You are not being tested on your
ability to guess the vocabulary — if you notice something real that is not
in here, that is a finding about the system and the most useful thing you
can write down.


HOW A MISTAKE IS DECIDED AT ALL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Every one of your moves is compared with the engine's best move at depth 15,
and the difference is converted to WIN PROBABILITY, not centipawns.

    inaccuracy      5 points of win probability given away
    mistake        18
    blunder        30

Three consequences worth knowing while you annotate:

  * FORCED SEQUENCES ARE INCLUDED. The engine's evaluation already accounts
    for anything forced inside its search, so a move that loses a rook six
    moves later, or allows a mate in three, registers on the move that
    ALLOWED it. You do not need to count material yourself, and the swarm
    never counts material — it reads the evaluation.

  * Evaluations are clamped at +-1000cp first, so mate is treated as
    'completely winning' rather than as infinity. Allowing mate in 1 and
    allowing mate in 10 therefore score the SAME, and going from already
    winning to mate scores nothing at all.

  * Anything below 5 points carries no label. A move giving away 9
    points of win probability is real and is not counted as an error. If you
    are noticing small errors, you are working finer than the swarm does
    (measured in E31).


THE SEVEN SECTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  S1  tactics
    claims: missed_motif / allowed_motif
    For every move, the engine's best move is checked for a tactical pattern.
    MISSED  = the pattern was there, you played something that lost ground.
    ALLOWED = your move was an error, and the opponent's best reply plays
              the pattern against you.

  S2  decision process
    claims: time_pressure / instant_move / long_think / time_budget
    The same errors, sorted by the CLOCK rather than by the board: what were
    you doing with your time when you went wrong.

  S3  endgames
    claims: endgame_error / advantage_error
    Errors once the position is an endgame, split by material left, and errors
    made while already clearly better.

  S4  openings
    claims: early_error / opening_disadvantage
    Errors before move 15, split by colour; and how often you leave the opening
    already worse.

  S5  pawn structure
    claims: concedes_weakness
    Moves after which YOUR OWN pawn structure has a new weakness in it.

  S6  squares and files
    claims: allows_square
    Moves after which the opponent has a square or file they did not have.

  S8  attack and defence
    claims: allows_pressure
    Moves after which more enemy pieces bear on your king.


EVERY SENTENCE IT CAN SAY ABOUT YOU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Verbatim from the report templates. If your note matches one of these,
the swarm has a name for it; if it does not, it may still have SEEN the
mistake without being able to name it.

  advantage_error
      You go wrong more often than most when you are already better.
  allowed_motif
      When you go wrong, it is often a {subject} that punishes you.
  allows_pressure
      Attacks build against your king more readily than against players at your level.
  allows_square
      You let opponents establish {subject} more often than players at your level.
  concedes_weakness
      You end up with {subject} pawns more often than players at your level.
  early_error
      You go wrong early {subject}.
  endgame_error
      Your play falls off in {subject} endgames.
  instant_move_error
      Moves you play instantly go wrong more often than they should.
  long_think_error
      Your long thinks tend to end in a mistake.
  missed_motif
      You miss {subject} tactics that were available.
  opening_disadvantage
      You come out of the opening worse more often than most.
  time_budget_error
      When you use up most of your clock before move 15, the rest of that game goes worse — the cost of a long think lands later, not on the move itself.
  time_pressure_error
      Your play falls off when the clock is short.

  ...and where a claim covers everything of its type at once:

  allows_square (pooled)
      You let opponents settle pieces into your position more often than players at your level.
  concedes_weakness (pooled)
      Your moves leave weaknesses in your own pawn structure more often than players at your level.
  early_error (pooled)
      You go wrong early, before the middlegame starts.
  endgame_error (pooled)
      Your play falls off in the endgame.
  opening_disadvantage (pooled)
      You come out of the opening worse more often than most.


THE TACTICAL PATTERNS IT KNOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

These eight and no others. Both directions: missing one, and allowing one.

  fork                     (fork)
  pin                      (pin)
  skewer                   (skewer)
  discovered attack        (discoveredAttack)
  hanging piece            (hangingPiece)
  free pawn                (hangingPawn)
  back-rank mate           (backRankMate)
  capturing the defender   (capturingDefender)
  trapped piece            (trappedPiece)

  pawn structure it can concede : backward, doubled, isolated
  squares it can give away      : a knight you cannot chase away, a rook on your second rank


THE CLOCK CONDITIONS IT SORTS ERRORS INTO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  instant move     played in 2 seconds or less
  time pressure    60 seconds or less left on the clock
  long think       3x your own typical time for that game
  time budget      the rest of a game after overspending by move 15


WHAT IT IS BLIND TO
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

Written down so you can tell a gap from an oversight. If you notice one of
these, the swarm will not have it, and saying so is still worth the line.

  why you lost the material
    The swarm records WHAT punished you, never WHY you allowed it. Undefended
    pieces left standing, a piece moved to an attacked square, an exchange
    counted wrongly, a defensive resource missed — none of these are
    measured. Only a probe (V9) asks, and only about a shortlisted finding.

  piece quality
    "Gave up a good bishop for a bad knight" has no detector. Nothing in the
    swarm represents a piece being good or bad.

  small mistakes, pawn-sized ones especially
    A free pawn now HAS a name ('free pawn', added in E32) — but a move has to
    cost 5 points of win probability before any pattern detector runs on
    it at all, and dropping one pawn in a balanced position frequently costs
    less. Measured across three reviewed players, 24 of 45 pawn notes sat
    below that line. Write them down anyway — the gap between what you
    notice and what the swarm labels is itself the measurement.

  what was LOST, as opposed to what won it
    The swarm names the mechanism, you tend to name the outcome. A pawn dropped
    to a pin is 'a pin' to it and 'a lost pawn' to you, and both are right.
    Where you can, say which piece or pawn went and to what — the two
    vocabularies can then be lined up instead of disagreeing.

  plans and ideas
    No detector represents a plan, a wrong plan, or playing without one.

  openings by name
    Games carry their ECO code, and nothing diagnoses a specific line.

  whether the opponent was any good
    Opponent rating is not used anywhere in the diagnosis.
